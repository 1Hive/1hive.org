## Beneficiary and problem

A person considering a Honey-related proposal, and their agent, need to distinguish what is requested, what has been decided, and what happened. Sources may be split across discussions, repositories, governance interfaces, and chain records.

Those distinctions are documented in [[Improvement proposals]] and [[Navigating core community infrastructure]]. The hypothesis that a single brief would reduce review effort still needs testing. No interviews or measured time savings are claimed.

## Why this belongs in 1Hive

Better evidence supports Honey stewardship. A reusable read-only brief also gives newcomers and agents a concrete way to participate in review. The output could become a tool, a public explainer, and a structured example for the [[Knowledge commons|knowledge commons]].

## Evidence and existing work

The local website source reviewed on 10 November 2026 includes separate decision, funding, and delivery fields in `proposals/TEMPLATE.md`, a proposal browser in `scripts/proposals.ts`, and source-checking guidance. These are repository features, not proof of live deployment or a verified Honey integration.

Before implementation, check the latest [website work](https://github.com/1Hive/1hive.org/pulls), [repository issues](https://github.com/1Hive/1hive.org/issues), and the selected deployment or tool. Extend an existing reader or record where possible. Current duplicate-work checks and selection of a suitable public Honey example remain unfinished in this illustrative concept.

## Contribution and alternatives

Prepare one manual brief containing the question, sources, observation time, requested outcome, and distinct decision, funding, and execution fields. Show missing or conflicting evidence explicitly.

Reuse the existing proposal format first. A focused addition to the proposal browser is another option. Build an adapter only if the experiment establishes a repeated need. Wallet signing, automatic voting, and a general dashboard are out of scope.

## First experiment and finish line

Within an agreed effort limit, produce one source-linked brief and test fixtures for stale data, inaccessible sources, a discussion without a decision, and a decision without execution evidence. Use a clearly labeled historical record or fixture if current state cannot be verified.

If a reviewer agrees, compare their answers from the brief with answers from the original sources. Record disagreements and review effort. Continue if the brief preserves the distinctions and makes review easier. Revise or stop if it hides uncertainty, duplicates a sufficient tool, or costs more to maintain than the repeated work it removes. No trial has run yet.

## Review, maintenance, and authority

A reviewer familiar with the selected mechanism and a newcomer would be useful; neither is assigned. An adapter would need source-version handling, a refresh policy, and a maintainer. These remain unconfirmed.

No funding is requested or approved. Start with independent research and a private prototype within the contributor's permissions. Publication and contacting reviewers are separate actions. Later contract or governance changes require research, specialist review, and the applicable decision process.
