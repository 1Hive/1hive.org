## Put new productive capacity to work for everyone

AI automation can lower the cost of producing some kinds of research, software, analysis, and other useful work. It also raises a harder question: who will own the resulting capacity, choose what it is used for, and share in its benefits?

The same transition could destabilize livelihoods and existing institutions, or concentrate power in a small number of platforms. 1Hive's response can be practical: build decentralized institutions that help people coordinate around shared needs, govern common resources, and leave useful infrastructure in the public domain or under open licenses.

This is a proposed direction for the next chapter of 1Hive, grounded in its [[Our mission and values|existing public-goods mission]]. It is an invitation to build and evaluate, not a claim that a new protocol or funding program has been adopted.

## A commons of people, agents, and compute

People bring purpose, experience, judgment, relationships, and accountability. Agents can help research, implement, test, explain, and review. Contributors can voluntarily direct available compute toward work they consider valuable.

Pooling effort does not require pooling credentials. A person can run their own agent, keep control of their account and permissions, and contribute a result others can inspect. Any donated or subscription-backed capacity must be used with its owner's permission and within the provider's terms.

The aim is to turn distributed capacity into public value: maintained tools, reliable research, reusable data, and knowledge that compounds rather than disappears into individual chats.

## Where Honey fits

[[Why Honey?|Honey]] is the coordination asset at the center of 1Hive's public-goods economy. Useful contributions can strengthen the commons that Honey supports. Credible governance, transparent evidence, and responsible stewardship can in turn make it easier to direct resources toward more useful work.

That connection needs to be demonstrated through outcomes. No contribution is automatically entitled to Honey, and an AI-generated output is not valuable merely because it consumed compute. Funding should be connected to a clear public benefit, reviewable results, realistic costs, and the applicable decision process.

Start with a bounded experiment using resources its contributor is willing and authorized to offer. If the result is useful and more capacity would make a difference, use the evidence to explain what additional support would buy.

## Three ways to contribute

- **[[pathways/Core infrastructure|Build the core]]:** Honey tooling, agents, skills, documentation, and research that makes coordination and governance more capable.
- **[[pathways/Communications and narrative|Make the work legible]]:** explain the purpose, show the evidence, and help people find their place in the work.
- **[[pathways/Tangible public goods|Produce public goods]]:** answer meaningful questions and create open resources people beyond 1Hive can use.

These lanes reinforce one another. Better tools make useful work easier. Useful work gives the story substance. A clear story helps more people contribute. A curated [[Knowledge commons|knowledge commons]] could preserve the results and make each round of work a stronger starting point for the next.

## What we can start learning now

Which contributions benefit from agent assistance? What review catches errors without consuming all the time automation saves? Which resources remain useful beyond a single experiment? How should a shared body of knowledge be curated and governed?

We can investigate those questions through real contributions without pretending the eventual protocol, incentives, or licensing model is settled. Begin with [[First contribution launchpad|the contribution launchpad]] or [[Propose a contribution|bring your own concept]].
