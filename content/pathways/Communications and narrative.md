## The lasting question

How do we make the purpose and progress of a public-goods economy clear enough that people can understand it, challenge it, and decide to contribute?

The story becomes stronger when it connects an ambition to something real: a useful tool, a research result, a transparent decision, or a contributor's experience. Communication can itself be a public good when it leaves durable understanding.

## Existing foundations

Start with [[Our mission and values]], [[Why Honey?]], and the proposed [[Why now|AI-era direction]]. Use original [project work](https://github.com/1Hive) and [issues](https://github.com/1Hive/1hive.org/issues) or [pull requests](https://github.com/1Hive/1hive.org/pulls) in the website repository for claims about activity. The [public-activity skill](https://1hive.org/skills/summarize-public-activity/SKILL.md) and [source-checking skill](https://1hive.org/skills/check-sources/SKILL.md) provide reusable workflows.

Read the actual artifact and latest relevant discussion. Distinguish an idea, a working prototype, a merged change, a deployment, and observed use. Historical material can explain the context without establishing current availability.

## Contributions you could originate

- **An essay or explainer.** Explain a concrete part of the thesis: why decentralized public-goods institutions matter in an age of automation, how Honey fits, or how a person can contribute their agent's work. Use examples and label aspirations clearly.
- **An evidence-backed dashboard.** Make contributions, research results, governance records, or delivery evidence easier to inspect. Show sources, dates, missing data, and the distinction between commitments and outcomes.
- **A contributor case study.** Document the problem, process, artifact, review, and learning from a real contribution. Include failures and unresolved questions, not only a polished success narrative.
- **A blog or social series around a real result.** Translate substantive work for a specific audience, then point people toward a useful next step. Prepare drafts before seeking publication approval.

These are contribution ideas, not a communications mandate or a claim of authority to speak for the DAO.

## First experiment and evidence

Pick one audience, one important question, and one output. Test whether a reader can explain the idea accurately and find the relevant source or contribution path. A useful next step or corrected misconception is stronger evidence than impressions alone.

Check factual claims and avoid promising funding, adoption, returns, or services that do not exist. Obtain consent for personal stories and permission for the material you reuse. Publishing, contacting contributors, or speaking from an official account requires the appropriate authorization.
