1Hive depends on the community’s ability to recognize useful work and direct Honey toward it.

If Honey is going to become a reserve asset for public goods, the community needs strong curation. That means noticing work that strengthens the network, evaluating whether it creates lasting value, and helping shared resources flow toward contributors and projects that advance the mission.

Curation is not just appreciation. It is part of capital allocation.

**Check current funding before making commitments.** The deployments below are reference links from the wiki, not confirmation of an active budget, open reward program, or available payment. Check the relevant proposal, pool configuration, balances, and decision process at the source. Useful work does not automatically create a claim to Honey.

## What should be funded

The standard is simple: does the work make 1Hive, Honey, or the broader commons more capable?

Examples include:

- maintaining governance or treasury infrastructure,
- improving Honey liquidity, utility, or distribution,
- building open-source tools others can reuse,
- writing documentation that makes participation easier,
- researching better governance or funding mechanisms,
- resolving disputes or reducing operational risk,
- helping contributors coordinate and ship useful work,
- creating public goods that strengthen the ecosystem around 1Hive.

Not every positive action needs funding. But work that creates durable value should be visible, evaluated, and, when appropriate, rewarded.

## The Common Pool

The **1Hive Common Pool** is the community-governed pool associated with the [Gardens v1 deployment](https://gardens.1hive.org/#/xdai/garden/0x8ccbeab14b5ac4a431fffc39f4bec4089020a155). Verify its current balance, rules, and proposal status in the deployment before planning a request.

A funding proposal should explain:

- what will be built or delivered,
- why it matters for 1Hive or public goods,
- how much Honey is requested,
- who will receive it,
- what milestones or evidence the community should expect,
- what risks or tradeoffs are involved.

Use the [1Hive website repository](https://github.com/1Hive/1hive.org) for public reasoning and the relevant governance interface for the actual request and decision. Check submission requirements and execution conditions first. Discussion, a repository merge, and approval of a funding request are separate events.

If your work only needs a website or documentation change, start with the normal contribution workflow. The [[Improvement proposals|working-draft improvement process]] is available for a larger plan; it does not approve spending.

## Gardens v2 pools

The wiki also documents modular conviction voting experiments in **Gardens v2**. Different pools have been described for signaling, contributor rewards, liquidity support, and other targeted allocation processes.

Referenced pools include:

- **[Citizens Registry](https://app.gardens.fund/gardens/100/0x71850b7e9ee3f13ab46d67167341e4bdc905eef9/0xe2396fe2169ca026962971d3b2e373ba925b6257/5)**: a reference for community allowlist signaling.
- **[Contributor Rewards](https://app.gardens.fund/gardens/100/0x71850b7e9ee3f13ab46d67167341e4bdc905eef9/0xe2396fe2169ca026962971d3b2e373ba925b6257/12)**: a reference for contributor-support signaling. A signal is not evidence that a reward stream is funded or running.
- **[Honey Liquidity Tap](https://app.gardens.fund/gardens/100/0x71850b7e9ee3f13ab46d67167341e4bdc905eef9/0xe2396fe2169ca026962971d3b2e373ba925b6257/27)**: a reference for liquidity proposals.
- **[Council Safe Continuous Election](https://app.gardens.fund/gardens/100/0x71850b7e9ee3f13ab46d67167341e4bdc905eef9/0xe2396fe2169ca026962971d3b2e373ba925b6257/6)**: a reference for council membership signaling.

Before relying on any pool, verify its current purpose, eligibility, decision rules, activity, available funds, and how an approved signal is executed. These references do not establish that a pool is active or accepting requests today. Allocation experiments should be judged by whether they improve the community's ability to fund useful work and steward Honey.

## How to curate well

Good curation is concrete.

When supporting work, explain:

- what was delivered,
- who benefits,
- whether it can be reused,
- how it strengthens Honey, governance, infrastructure, or public goods,
- whether the requested reward or budget is reasonable.

When questioning work, be specific. Ask for evidence, milestones, scope, costs, risks, or clearer ownership. Curation should make decisions better, not simply make contributors feel judged.

## Why it matters

1Hive’s long-term goal is to fund more public development than any other organization.

That only works if the community can allocate Honey better than centralized institutions allocate capital. Curation is how we build that capacity: one proposal, contribution, review, and funding decision at a time.
