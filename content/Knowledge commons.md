title: Knowledge commons

## Make every useful contribution a better starting point

Research, code, discussions, and decisions become more valuable when the next contributor can find the relevant context, understand its limits, and build on it. A shared knowledge commons could help 1Hive turn scattered outputs into durable collective capacity.

**This is an exploratory direction, not an adopted protocol or running service.** A manageable first experiment could focus on 1Hive's own public knowledge before attempting a broad research commons.

## More than a pile of documents

The useful unit is a claim or artifact with context: its source, date, version, status, license, review history, and relationships to other work. Retrieval should help a person or agent distinguish current guidance from history, evidence from interpretation, and a proposal from a decision.

Curation matters as much as collection. The design needs ways to correct errors, show disagreement, retire stale guidance, prevent spam, and make editorial authority visible. Unresolved or consequential questions need human escalation rather than a confident synthesized answer.

## A first experiment we could try

Choose a small set of public 1Hive sources and a handful of questions contributors actually ask. Build a source-linked, structured collection and compare retrieval with the current wiki and manual search.

Useful tests include:

- Can a newcomer find the right starting point without confusing a proposed service with a running one?
- Can an agent cite the exact source and explain what remains unverified?
- Does a correction propagate without erasing the historical record?
- Are conflicting sources and unavailable evidence visible?
- Can someone export or reuse the collection within its licenses?

Start with permitted public material. Private chats and account access are not automatically part of a shared corpus.

## Questions to design in public

Who can contribute and curate? How are disputed edits resolved? What does review establish? Who maintains the index and pays its costs? How can contributors leave or fork the shared resource? How should quality be evaluated without rewarding volume or creating entrenched gatekeepers?

Possible licensing or paid-access arrangements for some future uses are open questions. No licensing model, exclusive rights, or revenue-sharing mechanism is selected here. Any design must respect the rights and permissions of source contributors and preserve clear access terms for the material it includes.

A focused retrieval experiment, curation workflow, provenance format, or evaluation set could be a meaningful contribution now. [[Propose a contribution|Shape a concept]] rather than assuming the whole institution needs to be designed first.
